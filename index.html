Subscripta privacy policy

Last updated: 17 November 2026

Subscripta is a subscription "wallet": it helps you see what you pay for, what renews soon and what you might cancel. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: everything stays on your phone. We have no servers, no accounts and no analytics, and we never see your data.

What the app stores

The app stores your wallet and preferences, on your device:

  • the subscriptions in your wallet: service and plan name, price, the currency it is billed in and price history, billing cycle, next charge date and the day of the month it bills on, where it is billed (directly, Google Play, Apple or other), category, colour, a payment-method label such as "Visa •• 4821" (never a full card number), the account email or username and a short note if you add them, trial, pause and cancel state, shared seats and reminder settings;
  • your optional usage-tracking enrollment and start time for each card, and the days you log; stopping tracking keeps earlier logs;
  • exchange rates you type yourself for cards billed in another currency, and the day you set each one; the app never looks a rate up;
  • for each payment label you choose, the month that bank card expires and when you set it, so the app can remind you 30 days before; nothing else about the bank card is stored. A label is a short name you type: never enter a card number, and the app refuses a label that looks like one;
  • for each card you mark as cancelled, whether to ask the day after its access ends if the charge stopped, and your answer ("not charged" or "charged"); the app never checks with the provider or your bank;
  • how each free trial you guarded ended (cancelled, kept, or charged before you decided), for the Trial guard line in Insights;
  • one snapshot of your wallet for each month that ends, from the month you install this version: its monthly average, what its cards charged that month, how many cards were live and the average per category, for the trend in Insights. It is saved automatically, goes into your backups, and goes when you erase all data. The app keeps the last 60 months and never reads your bank;
  • when the app last checked your reminders in the background, and whether you turned on on-time delivery for Trial guard; both stay on this phone and are left out of backups;
  • whether App lock is on and how long the app may stay in the background before it asks again, and whether "Hide in recents and block screenshots" and "Hide amounts outside the app" are on (all off unless you turn them on); these stay on this phone, are left out of backups and go when you erase all data;
  • a record of the last 20 errors the app ran into, for Share diagnostics: when each happened, the app version, the Android version and phone model, the error's technical description and the screen it was on. Card names, amounts, accounts, notes and payment labels are masked out before a record is kept. It stays on this phone, is left out of the backup files you export, and goes when you clear it in Settings or erase all data;
  • your preferences (theme, wallet currency, monthly budget, reminder settings, and whether the monthly recap notification is on; it is off unless you turn it on).

This data is kept in the app's private storage. Other apps can't read it. So that nothing is lost by accident, the app also keeps one earlier copy of your wallet on the phone when it can't read the current one (it was damaged, or written by a newer version) and when you restore a backup over it, so the earlier wallet can be put back. When restoring a backup, this earlier copy also includes your usage logs so they can be restored with the wallet. Uninstalling the app, clearing its storage or Erase all data deletes all of it, unless you have exported a backup.

Onboarding and the full version

You add your own subscriptions. Subscripta does not read email or connect to a bank. The optional full-version purchase is simulated on this device. It unlocks usage comparisons, price increase reports and spreadsheet export. It collects no payment details and makes no charge.

What leaves your device

Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else. The app makes no network requests of its own: exchange rates are typed by you and never fetched. In particular, it has:

  • no account or sign-in;
  • no analytics, crash reporting or advertising SDKs;
  • no tracking across apps or websites.

Some things you do in the app use other parts of Android, under their own policies:

  • The cancel checklist's first step opens the service's own account page in your browser (a Chrome Custom Tab) when the app knows it. For a card you marked as billed through Google Play or Apple it opens Google Play's or Apple's subscriptions page instead. For any other service it opens a Google search for "cancel {service} subscription", so Google receives the service's name as a search. The website you visit sees that visit as it would any other. The app only opens the page: you cancel there, and the app records what you tell it.
  • Reminders are scheduled on your device by Android. Their text (a service's name, price and renewal date) appears in your notifications and, depending on your settings, on your lock screen.
  • The daily background check runs on your phone about once a day, also while the app is closed, when Android chooses: it moves charge dates on, sets up the next reminders, refreshes the widgets and, once a month has ended, saves its snapshot. It runs on the phone only and sends nothing.
  • Add to calendar (Trial guard) opens your calendar app's new-event screen, filled in with the service, your decision time and when the trial ends. Subscripta reads and writes no calendar: the event exists only if you save it there, under that calendar's own settings and policy.
  • Home-screen widgets show your monthly total and upcoming renewals on your home screen.
  • App lock (Settings → Privacy and lock), off unless you turn it on, asks for your fingerprint, face or screen lock when the app opens and after it has been in the background for the time you choose. Android does the check: the app receives only whether it succeeded, never your fingerprint, face or PIN. It is a lock on the screen, not encryption: your data is stored as before, and a locked backup is how a file is encrypted. Two further switches there are separate from the lock: Hide in recents and block screenshots shows a blank page in Android’s recent apps and blocks screenshots and screen recordings of the app, and Hide amounts outside the app shows "•••" for amounts in the widgets and leaves amounts out of reminders.
  • Export a backup (Settings → Data) saves everything above into a file in a folder you choose through Android, or optionally opens the share sheet for another app. Subscripta rereads a saved file before confirming it; closing the share sheet does not confirm a saved copy. You can lock the file with a passphrase: it is encrypted on your phone (AES-256-GCM, with a key made from your passphrase by PBKDF2) and can't be read without it. The passphrase is never stored or sent anywhere, so a forgotten one can't be recovered. Without a passphrase anyone who gets the file can read it. Shared files stay in private cache for about an hour while the app is running so the receiving app can read them later. If Subscripta is closed, expired cache is removed on its next start or return; Android may clear cache sooner. Erase all data also removes these cached exports. Files you save and copies other apps keep are yours to manage: they remain after erasing or uninstalling Subscripta and must be deleted where you kept them. A cloud folder or share target you choose follows that provider's policies. Restore from a backup reads a file you pick and asks before replacing a wallet that has cards.
  • Export cards as a spreadsheet (Settings → Data) writes each card's name, plan, price, currency, billing cycle, next charge, category, status, trial end and where it is billed, and an approximate monthly amount in your wallet currency at your rates, into a CSV file and hands it to the share sheet the same way. It isn't encrypted.
  • Import cards from a spreadsheet (Settings → Data, or the empty wallet) reads a CSV file you pick, on your phone. Nothing is uploaded, and the file is read once and not copied or kept. The cards you tick on the review page are added to your wallet; nothing in it is replaced.
  • Share diagnostics (Settings → About) writes the error records above into a text file and opens the share sheet, so you can send it to us when something goes wrong. Nothing is sent unless you pick where it goes there, and the file contains no wallet data.
  • Android backup. If you have turned on backup for your Google account, Android may include the app's data in your device backup, which Google stores and encrypts under your account, and copies it when you move to a new phone. The schedule of reminders is left out: the app sets the reminders up again from your wallet. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings.

Children

Subscripta is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect anything from anyone, children included.
